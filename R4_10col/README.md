# G328: an exact 10-chromatic distance graph in R⁴

G328 extends a construction of the 65-vertex graph of
[Geoffrey Exoo, Dan Ismailescu and Michael Lim (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-014-9612-7).
Their paper establishes the bound χ(R⁴) ≥ 9. 

We improve this bound to χ(R⁴) ≥ 10 by adjoining all vertices in $Z[\sqrt{5}]^4$ that have at least 8 neighbors in G65.

Currently, work is underway on minimizing the graph and shortening the formal proof.

# AI disclosure 

The graph was constructed using GPT-6 Astra in response to a request proposing to improve 
the construction from the paper [EIL](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-014-9612-7). In fact, the result was obtained in the first 
reply: one of several proposed examples turned out to be successful, despite the fact 
that GPT-6 uses very limited CPU resources during its initial check. Verification required 
~50 hours of running the single-threaded SAT solver [Kissat](https://github.com/arminbiere/kissat) on a local computer (1 CPU core, ~4.5GHz Ryzen 5700X).

This repository was also mostly generated by GPT-6.

# Construction and relation to Exoo–Ismailescu–Lim graph G65

## Source and explicit correction

Section 5 uses S4 coordinate orbits and forbidden distance 4√2.

In the inspected published Table 2 (p. 422), the fifth seed starts with
$1+\sqrt{5}$. This repository instead uses $1-\sqrt{5}$, which agrees with the
corresponding row of Table 1 (p. 421). 

Our exact computation from the literal Table 2 yields 65 vertices, 472 edges,
and four isolated vertices. The corrected version yields 65 vertices and 588
edges, matching the paper's stated size. 

## Ten orbits

Put s=√5. Take every distinct coordinate permutation of each row below and
form their union. The orbit sizes sum to 65.

| Row | Seed | Orbit size |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | (1+s, 1+s, 1+s, 1+s) | 1 |
| 2 | (-4, 0, 0, 0) | 4 |
| 3 | (4, 0, 0, 0) | 4 |
| 4 | (2s, -2, -2, -2) | 4 |
| 5 | (1-s, -1+s, -1+s, -1+s) | 4 |
| 6 | (-1-s, -1-s, 1+s, 1+s) | 6 |
| 7 | (1+s, 1+s, -3+s, -3+s) | 6 |
| 8 | (-2, 2, 2, 2s) | 12 |
| 9 | (0, -2+2s, 2+2s, 0) | 12 |
| 10 | (-1+s, -3-s, -1+s, 3+s) | 12 |


The source code stores a point as eight integers `[a0,b0,...,a3,b3]`, denoting
the four coordinates `a_i+b_i*sqrt(5)`. Coefficients have no implicit denominator.

For a coordinate difference `(a_i+b_i√5)_i`,

\[
\|a+b\sqrt5\|^2=\sum_i(a_i^2+5b_i^2)+2\sqrt5\sum_i a_i b_i.
\]

It equals 32 exactly when the rational coefficient is 32 and the irrational
coefficient is zero. No numeric tolerance is involved.

## From 65 to 328 vertices

Enumerate

\[
D=\{a+b\sqrt5\in\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]^4:
\sum_i(a_i^2+5b_i^2)=32,\quad \sum_i a_i b_i=0\}.
\]

Every summand is nonnegative, so `|ai|<=5` and `|bi|<=2` suffice for exhaustive
enumeration. The recursion in `scripts/exact.py` visits every allowed coordinate
tuple and retains exactly 2,008 vectors.

For `x` in `V65+D`, count its distinct distance-4√2 neighbors in `V65`. Counting
the pairs `(v,d)` with `x=v+d` counts those neighbors exactly: for fixed `v,x`,
the difference `d` is unique. Retain all original vertices and every candidate
with count at least eight:

\[
V_{328}=V_{65}\cup\{x\in V_{65}+D:
|\{v\in V_{65}:\|x-v\|^2=32\}|\ge8\}.
\]

This gives 328 distinct points and 5,066 edges. The 65-vertex graph is an
induced subgraph, because both graphs include every pair at squared distance 32.
The vertex support rule and all edge decisions are verified by the scripts.
The support-eight extension is a construction of this project, not a statement
of the 2014 paper.

## Symmetry breaking

The main CNFs contain only one-hot coloring constraints and positive unit
assignments. They use **2,952 variables and 57,739 clauses each**.

All cases fix the clique K5 = $\{p_1, p_2, p_3, p_4, h\}$ (rows 3 and 1 in the table above).

```text
vertex IDs: 64 39 36 34 51
colors:      1  2  3  4  5
```

Then they fix the four negative-axis vertices $r_i$, $i=1,2,3,4$ forming the clique K4 (row 2).

Each $r_i$ is adjacent to $p_j$ exactly when $j \neq i$, and is not adjacent to h;
the color of $r_i$ can coincide with the color of $p_j$ only when $i=j$.
Therefore, the cases are classified according to the number of indices for which 
the colors $r_i$ and `p_i` coincide. In addition, it is necessary to keep track 
of the use of the color of the vertex h.

In particular, if there are no coincidences, then we obtain two cases: either K4 
contains a vertex of color 5, or there is no such vertex. 
Similarly for 1, 2, and 3 coincidences. 
If the colors of all four pairs of opposite vertices coincide, then the case is unique. 
Hence we have to deal with 9 cases:


| Case | color(0) | color(28) | color(31) | color(33) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| a0_b0 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| a0_b1 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| a1_b0 | **1** | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| a1_b1 | **1** | 5 | 6 | 7 |
| a2_b0 | **1** | **2** | 6 | 7 |
| a2_b1 | **1** | **2** | 5 | 6 |
| a3_b0 | **1** | **2** | **3** | 6 |
| a3_b1 | **1** | **2** | **3** | 5 |
| a4_b0 | **1** | **2** | **3** | **4** |


Thus **G328 is 9-colorable if and only if at
least one case is SAT**. All nine cases are retained. The proof and the full
automorphism-group certificate are in [symmetry](docs/SYMMETRY.md).

For vertex ID `v` (0 through 327) and color `c` (1 through 9), the DIMACS variable
is `9*v+c`. The original numbering is preserved throughout the main workflow.
